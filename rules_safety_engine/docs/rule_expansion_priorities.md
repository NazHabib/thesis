# Rule Expansion Priorities

## Priority 1 — Temporal and Longitudinal Rules

These are the most important next additions.

### Examples
- repeated elevated blood pressure across visits
- worsening HbA1c over time
- persistent high sodium intake across repeated recalls
- no improvement in lifestyle risk after follow-up interval
- escalation after repeated failure of lower-intensity recommendations

### Why this is highest priority
These additions would move the system from cross-sectional support toward clinically realistic follow-up reasoning and future-oriented decision support.

---

## Priority 2 — Follow-up Scheduling Rules

### Examples
- immediate review triggers
- short-term 1 month review logic
- medium-term 3–6 month reassessment
- annual maintenance follow-up
- follow-up timing based on severity and multimorbidity burden

### Why this matters
It strengthens the practical usefulness of the rulebase and connects directly to future recommendation scheduling features.

---

## Priority 3 — Richer Multimorbidity Coordination

### Examples
- hypertension + obesity integrated support
- diabetes + dyslipidaemia + lifestyle-risk bundles
- obesity + inactivity + dietary-risk interaction rules
- multimorbidity-sensitive escalation thresholds

### Why this matters
It improves coherence and realism in patients with clustered cardiometabolic risk factors.

---

## Priority 4 — Confidence- and Data-Quality-Aware Refinement

### Examples
- recommendation weakening under repeated missingness
- stronger caution messages under unreliable measurements
- longitudinal data sufficiency checks
- confidence-sensitive information rules

### Why this matters
It would further strengthen the system’s safety-first behaviour under imperfect real-world data.

---

## Priority 5 — Broader Fine-Grained Lifestyle Personalisation

### Examples
- more nuanced diet-pattern rules
- deeper lipid-pattern support
- behaviour-stage-aware smoking or alcohol advice
- more specific sedentary-risk combinations

### Why this matters
Useful, but lower priority than temporal and multimorbidity improvements.

---

## Summary

Future expansion should focus on **smarter rule dimensions**, not simply a larger number of rules. The strongest next-generation improvements are:

1. temporal logic
2. follow-up timing
3. multimorbidity coordination
4. data-quality-sensitive refinement

These priorities align with the dissertation’s safety-first hybrid architecture and provide a realistic roadmap for future system development.
